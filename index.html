                        How this mission works and what it does not prove +

                        The rover and its telemetry are a kinematic simulation, not a physical vehicle. In a site view, the surface combines a 2024 Sentinel-2 crop and 81 Copernicus GLO-90 elevations every 300 m over an approximately 2.4 km window. In the A.R.E.S. scenarios, both halves use 65×65 nodes every 37.5 m: Copernicus GLO-30 and Sentinel-2 L2A imagery on Earth; LOLA, Kaguya TC, HiRISE or CTX on the Moon and Mars. Relief is vertically exaggerated ×2; the rover is enlarged to be visible.

                        Approximate slopes are derived from the interpolated regional DEM. They do not identify rocks, loose sand or traversal risks at rover scale. Alerts use editorial thresholds, not validated vehicle limits. The geometric similarity index compares only terrain slope, relief and roughness measured in the same window: it does not reproduce gravity, regolith, illumination or chemistry, and its weights are editorial, not calibrated. DEMs do not validate test routes.

                        The ranking remains documentary. Coordinates are indicative, not measured polygons. Current Earth weather comes from the provider’s weather model, queried now or from an archive; it is not telemetry from a physical rover. Moon and Mars temperatures are Diviner or Mars Climate Database products, not live measurements. Sources in docs/qa/targets-sources.md. View data sources ↑
